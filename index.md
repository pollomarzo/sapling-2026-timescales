# Intrinsic timescales lengthen along a synthetic cortical hierarchy

+++ {"part": "abstract"}

Cortical regions are often described as forming a hierarchy of processing timescales, with
sensory areas integrating information over tens of milliseconds and association areas over
seconds. Here we ask whether that ordering emerges from a minimal generative model rather than
from anatomy. We simulate activity for eight regions as first-order autoregressive processes
whose memory increases along a single latent axis, then recover an intrinsic timescale from each
region's autocorrelation. The recovered timescales rise monotonically along the axis, spanning
roughly an order of magnitude, and the estimate is stable across simulation seeds. The result is
a small, fully reproducible demonstration that a one-parameter gradient is sufficient to produce
a timescale hierarchy, offered here as a worked example rather than a claim about real cortex.

+++

## Introduction

A recurring observation in systems neuroscience is that the timescale over which a region's
activity is correlated with itself grows as one moves from primary sensory cortex toward
association areas [@synthetic2026hierarchy]. Whether this gradient requires detailed circuit
differences or follows from a single underlying parameter is a question that a generative model
can make precise. We take the second view as a null model: one latent axis sets each region's
memory, and everything else is identical.

## Methods

We simulate eight regions, indexed by a hierarchical level $\ell \in \{1, \dots, 8\}$. Each
region's activity $x_\ell(t)$ follows a first-order autoregressive process,

$$ x_\ell(t) = a_\ell \, x_\ell(t-1) + \varepsilon(t), $$

with $\varepsilon(t)$ drawn from a standard normal distribution and the memory coefficient
$a_\ell$ increasing smoothly with level. From each simulated series we estimate the intrinsic
timescale $\tau_\ell$ by fitting a single exponential to the autocorrelation function, following
the standard procedure [@synthetic2026methods]. We repeat the simulation across twenty seeds and
report the mean and standard deviation per region.

## Results

The recovered timescales increase monotonically along the hierarchical axis, as the figure
below shows. The shortest-memory region sits near a few sampling steps, the
longest near forty, and the across-seed spread is small at every level, so the ordering is not an
artefact of a single simulation. A one-parameter gradient is therefore sufficient to reproduce
the qualitative signature that motivates the hierarchy account. On the web version, hover any
point in the figure to read its across-seed mean and spread.

## Discussion

The demonstration is deliberately minimal. Because the generative model contains a single axis of
variation, the emergent timescale hierarchy cannot be evidence for anything beyond that axis; it
shows only that the axis is sufficient, not that real cortex is organised this way. The value of
the example is reproducibility: the figure, the numbers behind it, and this text are produced by
one pipeline from source, which is the property we care about here.

## Data availability

All data are synthetic and generated by the model described above. The specification of the
figure and its underlying values are contained in this repository; there is no external dataset.
